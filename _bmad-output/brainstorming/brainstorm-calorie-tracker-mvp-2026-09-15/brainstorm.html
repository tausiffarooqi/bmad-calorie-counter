Technique 01 — Morphological Analysis (Deep)

Laying every ingredient on the counter

The product was broken into its independent parameters — a full pantry of design decisions — so each one could be considered on its own before deciding which combinations belong in the MVP.

1
Meal input mode
  • Photo upload
  • Free-text description (e.g. "one double cheeseburger and medium fries with a can of diet soda")
2
Calorie estimation pipeline
  • Photo → vision recognition → generated text description → calorie calc
  • Text → direct calorie calc
  • Insufficient detail (either mode) → re-prompt user to retry
3
Response composition per submission
  • Estimated calories of the meal just logged
  • Total calories remaining for the day
  • One meal recommendation per remaining meal of the day
4
Time-of-day meal-count logic
  • 5am–12pm submission → 2 recommendations (lunch + dinner)
  • 12pm–10pm submission → 1 recommendation (dinner)
  • Maps time windows to remaining meal slots
5
Entry classification
  • Meal vs. snack/beverage — auto-detected
  • Snacks/tea/coffee update remaining calories
  • ...but don't decrement the meal-recommendation count
6
Recommendation content driver
  • MVP: single account-level preference — vegetarian vs. non-vegetarian, set on the account page
  • Deferred: macros, cuisine, allergies, finer-grained preferences
7
Daily target & day boundary
  • Day runs on the user's local timezone, 5am to 12am midnight
  • Daily calorie target set by the user on a preferences page, defaulting to a standard adult intake
8
Sensitive-data surface
  • Daily target & actual intake are personal data the user may not want shared — even if not classic PII
9
Credential protection
  • Email/password login credentials need protection
10
Photo-content risk
  • Users might accidentally upload personal (non-meal) photos
  • Mitigate via an in-app warning to only upload meal photos
11
Threat model
  • Primary concern: bad actors harvesting personal data for ad-targeting/marketing — not just generic account takeover

11 parameters on the table — dashed cards (8–11) are the privacy/security cluster that later regroups as one MoSCoW bucket.

Two combinations put to the test

1
Entry Classification × Recommendation Driver

Snacks and beverages never get their own recommendation — the vegetarian/non-vegetarian driver only applies to meals. A snack's calories are simply subtracted from the remaining daily budget, shrinking the calorie allotment left for the remaining meal recommendations.

2
Time-of-Day Logic × Over-Target Handling

Refined windows closed the earlier gaps: 5am–12pm → 2 recommendations (lunch + dinner); 12pm–10pm → 1 recommendation (dinner). After 10pm, a recommendation is only given if the user hasn't yet met their daily target. And confirmed: once the user exceeds their daily target — at any time of day — recommendations stop entirely and the app instead reports the excess calories consumed.

Technique 02 — Job to Be Done (Structured)

Following the day's first ask to its emotional root

From the plain surface request down to what the user actually needs to feel — traced as one arc, from morning login to the alternative this app replaces.

Surface job
"Log what I ate, and tell me what to eat next."

Submit a meal by photo or text, get a calorie estimate, and find out what's left to eat for the rest of the day.

Deeper motivation
Replace guesswork with a concrete plan

Not obsessive tracking for its own sake — a want to feel steered toward a good decision, without having to do the math or the thinking.

Trigger moment
First login of the day

The app shows remaining calories and asks if the user wants to log a meal now. If they decline, it still shows recommendations for the remaining meals based on current progress.

If that first login is before 10am, it additionally asks whether the user wants a breakfast recommendation — confirmed as its own third meal slot, separate from the lunch + dinner pair.

Confirmed: breakfast is a distinct opt-in slot
Emotional tone
Coach, not accountant

The first-login message adapts to yesterday's performance: congratulatory and confidence-building if the user stayed within target, supportive and encouraging — never shaming — if they went over.

Social dimension
Deliberately none

Decision: no social or accountability features for now. This is scoped as a solo, private experience.

Status-quo alternative
What people do without this app: guess

Most people have no structured way to estimate what they've eaten, or to decide what to eat next. That guesswork is the real competitor.
